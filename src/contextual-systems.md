# Contextual Systems

## [Ontology](./ontology.md)

A contextual system is a [system](./system.md) whose [entities](./entity.md) take their meaning, truth, and behaviour from the [context](./context.md) that contains them, so that an [abstraction](./abstraction.md) in it can only be understood relative to a stated [scope](./scope.md). Contextualism is the doctrine that every system is contextual: the same word, [signal](./signal.md), or act means something different under a different context.

## [Axiology](./axiology.md)

Treating a system as contextual is what makes [definitions](./definition.md) usable across [domains](./domain.md): a term is defined at the broadest scope ([Existence](./existence.md)) and narrowed by the context it is used in, rather than forced to mean one thing everywhere. It is also what makes [communication](./communication.md) between contexts a translation rather than an argument, since neither side's meaning is wrong, only scoped. The risk is [relativism](./reality.md): a context must still be stated, and a claim that holds in no stated context holds nowhere.

## [Epistemology](./epistemology.md)

### [Cultural](./culture.md) Definition

<a href="https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/contextualism" target="_blank">contextualism (wiktionary)</a>

> (philosophy) Any of a group of doctrines that stress the importance of context

<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contextualism" target="_blank">Contextualism (Wikipedia)</a>

> Contextualism describes a collection of views in philosophy which emphasize the context in which an action, utterance, or expression occurs, and argues that, in some important respect, the action, utterance, or expression can only be understood relative to that context. Contextualist views hold that philosophically controversial concepts, such as "meaning P", "knowing that P", "having a reason to A", and possibly even "being true" or "being right" only have meaning relative to a specified context. Some philosophers hold that context-dependence may lead to relativism; nevertheless, contextualist views are increasingly popular within philosophy.

### [Pattern](./pattern.md) Expression

The contextual pattern appears wherever one entity is read under more than one scope:

- **Software**: a bounded context in domain-driven design; `customer` means one thing to billing and another to support, and the boundary is where the meaning changes.
- **Language**: a word's sense shifts with the sentence, the dialect, and the [culture](./culture.md); a dictionary lists contexts, not one meaning.
- **Signals**: the same bytes are a number, a character, or an instruction depending on the protocol that reads them.
- **Ethics**: an act judged differently by the [belief system](./belief-system.md) that frames it; contextualism names the frame instead of pretending there is none.
